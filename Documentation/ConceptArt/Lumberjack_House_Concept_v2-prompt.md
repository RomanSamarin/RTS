DESIGN, essential:
A modest medieval LUMBERJACK LODGE organized as an asymmetrical U-shaped working yard, entirely unlike a cottage. The dominant building is a LONG, LOW, OPEN-FRONT timber log hall at the REAR, supported by widely spaced enormous roundwood posts. Its continuous saturated muted cobalt / slate-blue gabled roof has a very low eave line and long ridgeline, visibly much longer than it is tall. Two or three genuinely open structural bays reveal orderly stacks of raw tree trunks and a manually operated sawbuck, NOT stalls or stall doors. All structure is exposed rough-hewn timber with pegged joints; very little plaster.
At the BACK RIGHT end, a SMALL enclosed workers' room projects toward the courtyard as the short right arm of the U. Its narrow BLUE single-pitch roof slopes in a different direction and its closed solid plank door faces INTO the courtyard, accessible via two stone steps. It is subordinate to the long hall, not a central tower or a tall cottage. One tiny square window only, no balcony, no tall chimney.
The LEFT arm is a separate LOW roofed drying rack, only waist-to-shoulder high, a simple blue-roofed log store. Leave generous openings between the three masses so it reads as an asymmetrical group of connected practical structures around a working yard, not a monolithic symmetrical barn. The whole FRONT is fully OPEN to a dirt road, with no fence, no gates and no archway.
A chopping stump, a simple sawhorse, one neatly stacked pile of raw trunks with visible circular ends, a two-man hand saw hanging on a post and a small two-wheel handcart make the function clear. A small crossed-axes timber sign is the only sign. Sparse purposeful props, clear walking path, no industrial machinery, no waterwheel, no workshop full of weapons, no fantasy decorations.
Overall scale modest, about the footprint of a small forestry workplace, with a couple of low-poly firs behind. Make the open canopy/log hall the dominant recognizable feature. A small lumberjack lifting a short log gives scale.
